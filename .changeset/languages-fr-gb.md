**Breaking:** a resume with a UK phone number (+44), a French resume, and a resume in a language with no pack are now read differently, so their recovered fields and scores change for the same input.

- **A French language pack (`fr`) and a France region (`FR`)**, in `BUILT_IN_LOCALES` and exported from `/locales`. A French CV is read with its headings (with and without accents: "Expérience professionnelle", "Competences", "Centres d'intérêt"), months and their abbreviations ("janv.", "févr."), "depuis", "aujourd'hui" and "à ce jour", ranges by "à" and "au", level words ("langue maternelle" C2, "courant" and "bilingue" C1, "notions" A2), licence, diplôme d'ingénieur and doctorat, title and school words, French injection phrases, and a posting's "Missions", "Profil recherché" and "Les plus". `FR` reads day-first dates and the French system's credentials (brevet, baccalauréat, CAP, BTS, DUT, BUT, Bac+2/+3/+5/+8) and does not judge a date of birth or a photo, as a French CV commonly carries both. A French resume was read with English rules: on our fixtures it missed most section headings, read a degree line as a job and recovered no skills; it now reads every field. Both are `community` until a native-speaking maintainer reviews them.
- **A United Kingdom region (`GB`).** A resume with a +44 number now reads all-numeric dates day first ("01/09/2019" is September 2019, where it was January), GCSEs and O-levels (level 2), AS-levels and Highers (3), HND, HNC and foundation degrees (5), PGCE and postgraduate diplomas (7) and Welsh "Coleg" schools, and weighs a stated date of birth or a photo as a warning, as the US region does (it was a light note with no region). A-levels and degree classes ("2:1", "First-class honours") read as before. `report.locale.region` is `"GB"` for such a resume, where it was `null`.
- **A language with no pack is no longer judged as English.** The policy now names its own language's common words (`text.detectionWords`) and those of eight languages it has no vocabulary for and thirteen scripts (`text.otherLanguages`), told by the same thresholds as a pack. A resume no attached pack recognises, written in one of those and not in English (a Spanish or Italian one, or a German one under the policy without packs), drops the rules limited to English (the eight writing checks) instead of passing or failing them on English words; its score moves by those rules. A language is told only by the words a resume has, never by the English it lacks, so every English resume, short or terse, is read as before. `report.locale` keeps its shape. Both fields are new in the policy schema, with defaults, so a policy written before them parses unchanged.
- Bundle sizes (gzipped): `/locales` from 71.4 to 75.4 KB, `/ai` from 72.1 to 72.6, `.` from 100.2 to 100.8, and `withLocales` alone from 48.1 to 48.7.
