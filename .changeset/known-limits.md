Fixes for the limits 0.17.0 shipped with, and for a few cases where it still read a resume worse than 0.16.0.

- **Breaking:** a leave or career break dated like a job is no longer read as a role, in text, in JSON Resume and ats-resume documents (also a JSON Resume entry named "Career Break" with no position), and in an AI repair. "Parental leave, 03/2024 – Present", "Career break 2019 – 2020", "Sabbatical", German "Elternzeit ab 03/2024", "Mutterschutz", "Auszeit" and French "Congé parental", "Année sabbatique" add no months of experience and no title for the seniority check. A German CV with 48 months of work and "Elternzeit ab 03/2024" read 80 months; it reads 48. A role that only mentions a leave ("Visiting Professor (sabbatical)", "HR Manager, Parental Leave Programs") is still a role, and so is a leave whose bracket or employer names a job title or an organisation: "Sabbatical (Visiting Researcher, Stanford)", "Sabbatical, Stanford University", "Elternzeit (Teilzeit als Entwickler bei Beispiel AG)". The words are a new policy list, `resumeParse.leaveWords`, which the German and French packs extend; the default policy's fingerprint changes.
- **Breaking:** a dated line that opens with a capitalised past-tense action verb, holds no job-title word before its dates and only lowercase words and numbers between the verb and them, is no longer read as a role when words or a full stop follow its dates, or when it ends in an everyday open end ("continuing", "onwards") after words of its own. "Analysed 2017 thru 2018 data", "Mentored two junior engineers since 2012.", "Maintained a legacy system originally built 2008 - 2011 by another team." and "Mentored 5 interns 2017 – continuing" each added a phantom role, and most added months of experience (36 → 129 months on one test resume). Headers that start like verbs ("Lead Engineer", "Managed Services Engineer", "Integrated Device Technology"), a line its dates end ("Founded startup 2017 – 2019", "Owned and operated a bakery 2015 – 2019", "Founded startup 2017 – Present", "Hired 2017 – 2019" under a title) and "Senior Engineer 2017 – continuing" are still roles. Other dated sentences are still read as roles, as in 0.17.0: one that opens otherwise ("Grew revenue every quarter from 2017 onward.", "Responsible for a codebase written in 2005 through 2009 and still in use."), one with a capitalised word before its dates ("Mentored two junior Java engineers since 2012."), and any line in a script without case, such as Hindi.
- **Breaking:** a tagline before a title is no longer read as the name, even when the email or a profile link spells it. "Open Source | Software Engineer" with github.com/open-source gave the name "Open Source"; it gives none, and a name on the next line is still read. The same holds for "Machine Learning", "Data Science", "Full Stack" and "Cloud Native", and for the "Surname, Given" form. The taglines are a new policy list, `resumeParse.notNames`, read only to find the name; job matching is unchanged. A name holding a skill's word ("Sean Go", "Go Min-jun", "Vue Nguyen", "Spark Lee", "Rust Cohle") is still read.
- **Breaking:** a "Languages" list of programming languages is not a list of spoken languages, wherever it is: under a "Languages" heading, on a "Languages:" line, or as a JSON Resume or ats-resume `languages` list. An item counts as a programming language when the policy knows it as a skill ("Python", "Go", "SQL", "TypeScript") or it holds a programming symbol ("C++", "C#", "Node.js"). Such a list no longer stops an English resume from meeting "Fluent English" at C1 from the language it is written in ("Languages" with "Python, Go" read unverifiable, and a JSON Resume with `languages: [Python, Go]` read missing; both are met), and gives no spoken-language rows. A list naming any spoken language ("English (B1), Python") still decides, and an item the policy does not know is a spoken language, as in 0.17.0: "ASL", "isiZulu", "IRISH", but also "HTML" or "GraphQL". This corrects the 0.17.0 note that such a list gives no credit.
- AI repair fills a phone number written with an extension word or a hash again, as 0.16.0 did ("+1 202 555 0143 extension 12", "+1 415 555 0134 #12"), and a toll-free vanity number ("1-800-FLOWERS", "1-800-GOT-JUNK"). A full toll-free number keeps its extension apart ("1-800-555-0199 EXT 12"); letters after any other number, or after a full one, still keep it out: "(555) 010-2020 CELL", "1-800-555-0199 CELL", "2019-2021 AB".
- Requirement rows count years per skill from the lines read as roles, so a dropped leave or sentence line does not shift them.
