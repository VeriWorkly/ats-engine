**Breaking:** role headers, headings and names that 0.4.0 read right and later versions read wrong are read right again, so recovered fields and scores change for the same resume.

- **An employer with its state's code keeps its employer and its title.** "Analyst at Deloitte, NY" beside its dates was read with no title and no employer; "Software Engineer, Google, CA", "Engineer | Intel | OR", "Data Analyst, Capital One, VA" and "Software Engineer" over "Google, CA" had no employer. A city of one or two words and a state's code is still where a role is worked, but not after "at" or "@" ("Analyst at Deloitte, NY" is at Deloitte), nor when a bar parts the code from it ("Engineer | Intel | OR" is at Intel). With commas only, or on a line of its own under a title, "Google, CA" is not told from "Austin, TX": when no other line names the employer (an employer line over the roles, or the role's own), its first part is the employer, as "Infosys, India"'s is. So "Senior Engineer, Austin, TX", which had no employer, is now at "Austin". After a bar or a dash ("Senior Engineer | Austin, TX") it is still where, and a line with a job title and a state's code ("Software Engineer, TX") is no longer taken for a place, which left the role with no title.
- **A list under a role keeps the roles after it.** "KEY PROJECTS", "SELECTED PROJECTS" or "TECH STACK" over bullets under a role, on a page of headings in capitals (or "Key Projects" on a page in title case), opened a section, so the next role was lost, and under "TECH STACK" its title, employer, dates and bullets were read as skills. A skills or projects heading inside the work history or the projects, with bullets right under it and a job title on the next line that is not a bullet, or on the lines down to its dates, is now a line of the role. A projects section that opens on a project ("Ledger Rewrite⇥2018"), or names no job title before its dates, still opens.
- **The name over its email under a list heading is the name.** A page opening with a list ("SKILLS" over "Python", or "LANGUAGES" over "English") and then the name over the email took the name from a name-shaped line over a job title further down: a reference ("John Smith" over "Engineering Manager, Acme"), an organisation ("Red Cross" over "Volunteer Coordinator"), a project, a school or a certification ("AWS Certified" over "Solutions Architect"), and the section that line was in was cut there, so the certifications were lost. Such a name directly over an email address is now the name, and the list above it ends there, as under a LinkedIn export's "Top Skills". A name-shaped first line under a heading, read as the name for want of another, no longer cuts its section: "CERTIFICATIONS" over "Google Cloud" over "Professional Cloud Architect" keeps both.
- **Education and certifications after a role open their section however they are set.** On a page of headings in capitals, "Education" in title case after the work history (or "Education:" on a page of headings without a colon) was read as a line of the last role, and the degree under it as a job ("BSc Computer Science" at MIT), adding its years to the work history. Only a skills, projects, languages or other heading is read as a line of a role for being set unlike the page's headings; the work history, education and certifications always open their section.
- **How long a role ran, between its header and its dates, leaves the header.** "Software Engineer, Acme Corp" over "2 years" (or "(2 years)", or "2 yrs 3 mos" under a stacked title and employer) over the dates was read with no title and no employer. The duration is skipped and the header is read above it.
- **An employer and its city beside the dates, over a title, is the employer.** "Acme Corp, Berlin⇥Jan 2020 - Present" over "Software Engineer" was read as the title "Berlin" at Acme Corp ("Acme Corp" at "Berlin" before 0.6.0), and "Infosys, India" or "Siemens AG, Munich" over a title as the title "Infosys" or "Siemens AG". A dated line with no title word, beside a line holding a job title alone, is now the employer and where, and the title is the line beside it: "Software Engineer" at "Acme Corp, Berlin" (no cities are known, so the city stays), at Infosys, at "Siemens AG, Munich". Without such a line, "Globex Corporation, Croupier" beside its dates is still the croupier at Globex Corporation.
- **A posting that offers sponsorship does not rule it out.** "Visa sponsorship is available for candidates who need sponsorship" and "We welcome applicants who require sponsorship" made a resume saying "Will require H-1B visa sponsorship" missing the right to work. A posting now rules sponsorship out only when it says so ("without sponsorship", "unable to sponsor"), or names a need for it with a negation ("Candidates requiring sponsorship will not be considered"); otherwise the requirement is unverifiable.
- **`analyze` redacts an email address with letters beyond ASCII or an apostrophe whole, and the parser reads it.** "zoë.müller@firma.de" and "josé.garcía@empresa.es" were sent to the model as written, "o'connor.pat@example.com" as "o'[EMAIL_3]", and the candidate's own "josé.garcía@correo.es" as "[LINK_1]@correo.es", read by the parser as "a@correo.es". An email's local part may now hold letters in any script and an apostrophe (not one first, which is a quote), in the parser's email and in what `analyze` redacts. SECURITY.md says so.
