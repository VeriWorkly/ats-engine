---
"@veriworkly/ats-engine": minor
---

Fixes from an audit of the engine.

- **Breaking:** text in a square-cornered bordered box or a tinted band of a PDF is no longer read as hidden text. A border filled as one frame, its outer rectangle with the inner cut out (`re re f*`), was taken for a solid box of its colour, and a translucent fill (`rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.06)`) for its full colour, so dark text in either failed the hidden-text check. A Chrome-printed resume with one tinted section scored 56 instead of 86; one with a table whose cells are framed so, 38 instead of 68. The inside of a border is now what lies under it, unless the border lies under most of the text: a small hole under the middle of a line does not make it visible. A translucent fill is blended with what lies under it; a fill that lets less than a tenth of what lies under it show counts as opaque, so a 99% white box over a picture, or four 50% layers, still hides white text. Translucent boxes of the text's own colour that together cover 70% or more of what lies beneath still hide it (black text on an 85% black box, or on three 50% ones); white text on an 85% navy band, black text on a 50% grey band and a chip tinted with its text's colour stay clean.
  - Still hidden: white text inside a border or on a faint tint, which was missed before.
  - A page splits at most 2,000 boxes from its filled shapes; past that, a shape is read as solid. A file drawing hundreds of intricate frames could otherwise stop the hidden-text check altogether.
  - Not changed: a rounded border (Chrome draws `border-radius` with curves) is still read as a solid box, so dark text in a dark rounded chip is still flagged.
- A PDF page the file declares millions of points wide is read in the time of any other page. The column search stepped across the declared width: a 50,000,000-point page took 12 seconds, so a small file of such pages held the reader for minutes. It now searches at most 14,400 points, the widest page a PDF allows.
- A document with many copies of one link is read in the time of any other. Each link's address was looked for in the whole text, once per copy: 2,000 copies over 2 MB of text took a second, and a small Word file of repeated links took minutes. Each address is now looked for once, among the text's words holding ".", "@" or "/", and the searches stop after 50,000,000 characters (a thousand searches of a full resume).
- An HTML page a Word document embeds (`w:altChunk`) is read once, however often the document names it, and the embedded pages share 8 MB between them. Each name read the page again, up to 8 MB each: one 2 MB page named 30 times took 1.6 seconds. A page past what is left is not read.
- **Breaking:** more ways of writing a current role's dates are read, so the role is no longer dropped, and the experience and seniority read from it no longer halve. A role dated "Jun 2021 - Till now" was missing from the work history, with no warning.
  - English: "Till now", "Continuing", "Jun 2021 onwards" (and "onward"), and "thru" between two dates.
  - A month with its zero and a two-digit year, between two such dates or before an open end: "06/21 - Present", "01/15 - 05/21". Alone, "10/10" is still no date, and "7/10 - 9/10" is a score. A span of two such dates must end the line or a column: "Rated 08/10 - 10/10 by users" is no role.
  - German: "– bis heute", "bis jetzt", "bis dato", and "ab 03/2020". Hindi: "अभी तक", and "मार्च 2020 से" with nothing after "से". French: "hiver" as a season (January to March).
  - "Continuing", "onwards" ("onward"), German "ab" and Hindi "से" are everyday words too: they date a role only where their date ends the line or a column. "Jun 2021 onwards" and "ab 03/2020" are current roles; "2020 onwards, growth of 30%" and "Jan 2017 continuing support for 3 apps" in a bullet are no role. "AB" in capitals is a company's suffix: "Volvo AB 2018 – 2021" is a role that ended in 2021. A since date still to come dates no role: "verfügbar ab 01/2027" is no current role. Any other open end may have a place after it: "Jun 2021 Present remote" and "03/2020 heute bei Siemens" are current roles.
  - **Breaking:** the default policy's `resumeParse.openEnded` gains "till now", "continuing", "onwards", "onward", "current role" and "current position", and `resumeParse.rangeWords` gains "thru". A new `resumeParse.lineEndDateWords` ("continuing", "onwards", "onward"; the German pack adds "ab", the Hindi pack "से") lists the open ends and since words that date a role only where their date ends the line. The default policy's fingerprint changes.
- **Breaking:** every kind of line break starts a new line: a lone carriage return (old Mac files, some Word exports), a vertical tab, a form feed, NEL, and the Unicode line and paragraph separators. Before, such a resume read as one line: an invented one scored 85 instead of 91, with its name and one of its two roles lost.
- **Breaking:** text in a PDF font that uses one of the standard CJK character maps (`UniJIS-UCS2-H`, `UniGB-UCS2-H` and the like, common in Chinese, Japanese and Korean resumes) is read. pdf.js was never told where its character maps are, so that text came out empty. Both PDF passes now get them, and the table pass no longer evaluates code from the file either (`isEvalSupported: false`, as the text pass already had).
- **Breaking:** AI answers are held to their fields' shapes. `mergeGrounded` fills a contact field only with that kind of value: an email address, its domain in any script (`jane@bücher.de`); a phone number's characters and 7 to 15 digits, read with its digits and dashes folded (Arabic-Indic or fullwidth digits, the non-breaking hyphen Word writes, an extension after a comma), and not a date range alone, years alone ("2019 2020 2021"), an IP address or an ISBN; a name of up to twelve capitalised words (pronouns in brackets and "3rd" aside), not a heading ("EXPERIENCE"), a sentence, one of the resume's own job titles or employers, or a phrase led by a job-title or organisation word ("Senior Engineer, Acme Corporation"); surnames such as Head, Bank or Cook are kept. A phone's label and note ("Mobile:", "(US)") are dropped before its shape is checked, and the number is stored without them. Any words of the resume used to pass, so a model, or text planted in the resume, could fill the email field with "Acme Corporation". `convertResume` compares a link whole, scheme and all: "https://fabrikam.ltd" no longer passes as the words "Fabrikam Ltd". A link the resume writes in brackets or guillemets ("[janedoe.dev]"), or with such marks in its path ("janedoe.dev/[draft]"), still counts.
- A provider's HTTP error no longer quotes its body in `AtsAiError`'s message: a gateway that echoes the request put the resume's name, email and phone there, and so into a host's logs. The message quotes only the provider's own error text (a JSON body's `error.message`, or a one-line text body, up to 200 characters, keys redacted), as in "Provider returned HTTP 400: Invalid value for 'temperature'.", else just "Provider returned HTTP 400.". Text broken by any line break, a lone carriage return or U+2028 included, is not quoted.
- **Breaking:** the right to work is compared by country. A statement naming only other countries than the posting asks about is no longer evidence: "Authorized to work in the US" or a green card no longer meets "right to work in the UK", and the row is unverifiable instead of met. A statement naming no country is read as before. "Must be a British citizen" is read as a citizenship ask, not as a skill. A heading or label alone ("Work Authorization") is no statement.
  - A citizenship meets a citizenship ask only for its own country, and each clause of a line is read for its own country: "Indian citizen; authorized to work in the US" shows a right to work in the US, not U.S. citizenship. "Latin American citizen" is no American citizenship. A bare "US" is read as the US beside "and", "&" or "/" ("Canada/US") and before a right-to-work word ("US Permanent Resident").
  - A list of countries is read past commas and brackets ("Authorized to work in the US, Canada and the UK", "Canada (PR) and the U.S. (TN visa)"), and a place the policy's countries do not name ("Ontario") leaves the line read as before. A nationality states that citizenship: "Citizenship: Indian", "British national", "EU passport", "Citizen of an EU member state", "Dual US-UK citizen".
  - Ireland is a country of its own: an Irish citizen may work in the UK and the EU, and a British or EU citizen in Ireland, but "Irish citizen" meets no U.S. or British citizenship ask.
  - **Breaking:** the default policy has a new `keywordMatch.requirements.countries`, each a `name`, its `patterns`, and optionally `citizensWorkIn` (other countries, by name, whose right to work its citizens hold): US (with "green card"; "us" only as "the US", "in the US" or "US citizen", never the pronoun), UK (with "Northern Ireland"), EU (with its member states), Ireland, Canada, Australia and India; US also reads "America" and "the States". A new `requirements.placeJoiners` lists the words that join places in a list. `requirements.authorization` and `requirements.citizenship` gain British, UK, EU, American, Canadian, Australian, Irish and Indian citizenship, and nationalities ("Indian national", "British passport holder"). The default policy's fingerprint changes.
- **Breaking:** a learner's language level no longer meets a fluent one. "Currently learning", "still learning" (A1), "school level" (A2) and German "Schulkenntnisse" (A2) are read, so "Spanish (currently learning)" no longer meets "Fluent Spanish", nor "Französisch (Schulkenntnisse)" "verhandlungssicheres Französisch". A level no word reads ("excellent") is still met, as a row with no level is. The default policy's `resumeParse.languageLevels` gains those three, and its fingerprint changes.
- **Breaking:** a negation about something else beside a clearance no longer cancels it. "Active TS/SCI clearance, no lapses since 2015" read as no clearance; it now meets "TS/SCI clearance required". "Active Secret clearance; Relocation: No" and "Active Top Secret clearance; not active duty military" meet it too. A negation counts in the clearance's own clause ("No active Secret clearance"), a "no" alone in the clause right after it ("TS/SCI clearance (no)", "Secret clearance: no"), and a negated phrase anywhere in its own sentence up to a semicolon: "Secret clearance (not active)", "TS/SCI clearance (not granted)", "…, not yet granted", "not in use since 2018", "Held TS/SCI clearance, not renewed". The default policy has a new `requirements.notHeldNearby` with those phrases ("not active", "not current", "not valid", "not held", "not obtained", "not renewed", "not cleared", "not (yet) granted", "issued", "approved", "in use", "no longer"); its fingerprint changes.
- **Breaking:** an expired, future-dated or in-progress certification no longer meets a requirement naming it, whatever words the requirement uses. "CISSP (expired 2021)" met "Active CISSP", "CISSP required" and "Must hold a current CISSP", and "CISSP (in progress, expected Dec 2026)" met "CISSP certification required". Such a requirement is now partial, with the row as the evidence and a detail saying why ("CISSP: expired 2021", "CISSP: not yet held"), unless a held row or the work history shows the skill. A row with no expiry is held, and an expiry needs an expiry word ("expired 2021", "valid until 2027"): a certificate dated by a plain range ("Google Data Analytics Certificate, Coursera, 2021 – 2022") is held. An expired certification named like a skill ("Kubernetes, expired 2022") does not hide that skill in the work history or skills list. In a one-line list, an in-progress word marks only its own item: "Six Sigma Green Belt (in progress), PMP, 2019" holds PMP, and "working towards AZ-104" or "renewal in progress" marks nothing unfinished. A certification listed twice, a lapsed row and its renewal, keeps its latest row, and "expired 2021, renewed 2023" is held. An in-progress word in a label ("Pursuing excellence: CISSP") does not mark the row unfinished; at the label's end ("Currently pursuing: CISSP") it does. The default policy has a new `requirements.inProgress`: "in progress", "pursuing", "studying for", "working towards"; `resumeParse.credentialWords.issued` gains "renewed" and "recertified".
- **Breaking:** a verb no longer meets the skill its stem spells. "Reacted to 40 outages" met React, "Sparked a redesign" Spark, "rusted runbooks" Rust, and "R&D" met R. A word folded onto a skill the policy spells as its own stem, by an ending other than a plural, stays itself ("APIs" still meets API, "Tested" still meets Testing). Two skills joined by "&" are each read ("C&C++" meets C, "AI&ML" meets AI and ML), and two single letters joined by "&" are one abbreviation: "P&L", "M&A" and "R&D" meet the same abbreviation, and "R&D" does not meet R. The default policy's `keywordMatch.phrases` gains "spark" and "rust", so they are named skills; its fingerprint changes.
- **Breaking:** the invisible-characters check no longer counts bidirectional embeddings (U+202A to U+202C), which Word writes around the words of a right-to-left document: an honest Hebrew or Arabic resume lost 10 points. They set a run's direction and reverse no letters. Overrides (U+202D, U+202E) and isolates around words are still counted.
- `analyze` no longer turns a reply that says nothing into an empty success. A reply wrapped in another key (`{"analysis": {…}}`), bare, in a fence or with prose around it, or with every field empty or blank (or only keyword suggestions when no posting was given), parsed as an empty analysis, with no error and no retry: a paid call, nothing back. It is now `invalid_output`, which is retried. A reply that shows a JSON example before its answer is read for the answer: each object in the reply is tried until one fits, and an object is read whole, never for something inside it. A 4.8 MB nested reply after prose is read in about half a second. A sparse answer (only keyword suggestions for a posting, say) is still one.
- A reply cut off at `maxTokens` whose answer is itself cut off, or cut off inside its reasoning, is `truncated`. One holding a complete inner object was read for that object, or retried up to 3 times as `invalid_output`, paying for the same cut-off reply each time. A reply cut off after its complete answer (a closed fenced block, or a whole object followed by prose) is still read.
- **Breaking:** a JSON Resume or `ats-resume` role whose end is an open end in words other than English "Present" is current. "heute", "aujourd'hui", "Current role" or "Till now" gave a role with no end and one month of experience (8 years read as 1 month, and "5+ years" as partial); an end that is an open end as a whole, in any language the policy and its packs read ("heute", "Current role", "- Present."), is now read as one. An end with more after the open end ("Present 2021", "Current role ended 2020", "Heute nicht"), one it cannot read ("soon", "Not current"), or Hindi "से" alone is still no end. Education entries read the same way.
- A huge paste is read without reading all of it: resume text is normalised 100,000 characters at a time until the 50,000 it keeps are filled, reading at most 2,000,000. A 40 MB paste with numbers took one to two seconds and its size in memory again; it now takes milliseconds. Invisible characters are counted on what was read and do not use up the 50,000.
