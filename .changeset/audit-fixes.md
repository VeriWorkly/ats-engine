Fixes from an audit of the engine.

- **Breaking:** text in a bordered box, a chip or a tinted band of a PDF is no longer read as hidden text. A border filled as one frame, its outer rectangle with the inner cut out (`re re f*`), was taken for a solid box of its colour, and a translucent fill (`rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.06)`) for its full colour, so dark text in either failed the hidden-text check. A Chrome-printed resume with one tinted section scored 56 instead of 86; one with a table whose cells are framed so, 38 instead of 68. The inside of a border is now what lies under it, and a translucent fill is blended with what lies under it.
  - Still hidden: white text inside a border or on a faint tint, which was missed before.
- A PDF page the file declares millions of points wide is read in the time of any other page. The column search stepped across the declared width: a 50,000,000-point page took 12 seconds, so a small file of such pages held the reader for minutes. It now searches at most 14,400 points, the widest page a PDF allows.
- A document with many copies of one link is read in the time of any other. Each link's address was looked for in the whole text, once per copy: 2,000 copies over 2 MB of text took a second, and a small Word file of repeated links took minutes. Each address is now looked for once, in the first 50,000 characters (what extraction keeps), and at most 1,000 distinct addresses are looked for.
- An HTML page a Word document embeds (`w:altChunk`) is read once, however often the document names it, and the embedded pages share 8 MB between them. Each name read the page again, up to 8 MB each: one 2 MB page named 30 times took 1.6 seconds. A page past what is left is not read.
- **Breaking:** more ways of writing a current role's dates are read, so the role is no longer dropped, and the experience and seniority read from it no longer halve. A role dated "Jun 2021 - Till now" was missing from the work history, with no warning.
  - English: "Till now", "Continuing", "Jun 2021 onwards" (and "onward"), and "thru" between two dates.
  - A month and two-digit year, between two such dates or before an open end: "06/21 - Present", "01/15 - 05/21". Alone, "10/10" is still no date.
  - German: "– bis heute", "bis jetzt", "bis dato", and "ab 03/2020". Hindi: "अभी तक", and "मार्च 2020 से" with nothing after "से". French: "hiver" as a season (January to March).
  - **Breaking:** the default policy's `resumeParse.openEnded` gains "till now", "continuing", "onwards" and "onward", and `resumeParse.rangeWords` gains "thru". The default policy's fingerprint changes.
- **Breaking:** every kind of line break starts a new line: a lone carriage return (old Mac files, some Word exports), a vertical tab, a form feed, NEL, and the Unicode line and paragraph separators. Before, such a resume read as one line: an invented one scored 85 instead of 91, with its name and one of its two roles lost.
- **Breaking:** text in a PDF font that uses one of the standard CJK character maps (`UniJIS-UCS2-H`, `UniGB-UCS2-H` and the like, common in Chinese, Japanese and Korean resumes) is read. pdf.js was never told where its character maps are, so that text came out empty. Both PDF passes now get them, and the table pass no longer evaluates code from the file either (`isEvalSupported: false`, as the text pass already had).
- **Breaking:** AI answers are held to their fields' shapes. `mergeGrounded` fills a contact field only with that kind of value: an email address; a phone number's characters and 7 to 15 digits, not a date range; a name without digits or "@". Any words of the resume used to pass, so a model, or text planted in the resume, could fill the email field with "Acme Corporation". `convertResume` compares a link whole, scheme and all: "https://fabrikam.ltd" no longer passes as the words "Fabrikam Ltd".
- A provider's HTTP error no longer quotes its body in `AtsAiError`'s message: a gateway that echoes the request put the resume's name, email and phone there, and so into a host's logs. The message quotes only the provider's own error text (a JSON body's `error.message`, or a one-line text body, up to 200 characters, keys redacted), as in "Provider returned HTTP 400: Invalid value for 'temperature'.", else just "Provider returned HTTP 400.".
- **Breaking:** the right to work is compared by country. A statement naming only other countries than the posting asks about is no longer evidence: "Authorized to work in the US" or a green card no longer meets "right to work in the UK", and the row is unverifiable instead of met. A statement naming no country is read as before. "Must be a British citizen" is read as a citizenship ask, not as a skill.
  - **Breaking:** the default policy has a new `keywordMatch.requirements.countries`, each a `name` and its `patterns`: US (with "green card"), UK, EU, Canada, Australia, Ireland and India. `requirements.authorization` and `requirements.citizenship` gain British, UK, EU, American, Canadian, Australian, Irish and Indian citizenship. The default policy's fingerprint changes.
- **Breaking:** a spoken language whose level is written but not read ("Spanish (some exposure)") is partial, not met, where the posting asks for a level, with the detail "Spanish: level not read, C1 asked". A row with no level at all is still met. "Currently learning", "still learning" (A1), "school level" (A2) and German "Schulkenntnisse" (A2) are read, so they no longer meet "Fluent Spanish" or "verhandlungssicheres Französisch". The default policy's `resumeParse.languageLevels` gains those three, and its fingerprint changes.
- A huge paste is cut before it is read through: resume text is cut to 100,000 characters, normalised, then cut to the 50,000 it keeps. A 40 MB paste with numbers took one to two seconds and its size in memory again; it now takes milliseconds. Text over 100,000 characters made mostly of invisible characters may now keep less than 50,000.
