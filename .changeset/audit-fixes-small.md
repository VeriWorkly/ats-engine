"@veriworkly/ats-engine": minor
---

Smaller fixes from the same audit.

- **Breaking:** "or related field" in a degree requirement no longer lets work experience stand in for the degree. A Bachelor's with 9 years is now missing against "Master's degree in Computer Science or related field", not partial ("…or equivalent experience"), and the ask gives the same result with or without "a". "Bachelor's in CS or a related field with 4+ years" needs the degree and 4 years of any work: 10 years and no degree is missing, and a B.S. Computer Science with 11 years is met. A field the ask abbreviates is held by the words the policy says it stands for (a new `keywordMatch.requirements.fieldAbbreviations`: CS, EE, AI, ML, HR, BA), read in the degree's own words: never by initials ("B.S. Chemistry, Stanford University" is not CS) or a school's name ("Colorado State", "Art Institute of Chicago"). A degree in a field the ask does not name is partial. The default policy moves that pattern from `keywordMatch.requirements.equivalence` to a new `keywordMatch.requirements.relatedField`, and adds `keywordMatch.requirements.fieldAbbreviations`; its fingerprint changes.
- **Breaking:** the German pack reads "-kenntnisse" compounds as languages, with their level. "Englischkenntnisse (C1)" and "sehr gute Französischkenntnisse" in a resume are language rows at their level. "Gute Französischkenntnisse" and "mit guten Englischkenntnissen" in a posting are language requirements at B1, not missing skills: a row of "Englisch (A1)" is missing it and "Englisch – Grundkenntnisse" is partial. "Basiskenntnisse" reads as A2 and "Muttersprachniveau" as C2. A language named twice in one ask is asked once, and is reported by its name ("Englisch"), not the compound.
- **Breaking:** a language requirement is met at C1 by the language the resume's own sentences (its bullets and summary) are written in, when the resume lists no languages, no line names that language, and at least three of its sentence lines (bullets, a summary) are written in it, most of them holding the language's own words, so one English summary over Spanish, Portuguese, Italian or Dutch bullets does not make an English resume. A "Languages" heading counts as a list of languages even when it lists programming languages ("Languages: Python, Go"), so it gives no credit. An English resume without a languages list now meets "Fluent English", and a German one "Fließend Deutsch"; both were unverifiable. It is never read as native: "Native English speaker" is partial. Headings and dates never count, so a Spanish resume with English headings, or a Portuguese JSON Resume, does not meet "Fluent English". A stated level such as "English: basic" or "English (A2)" still decides, and a language a host passes in `languages` is not credited unless the sentences show it.
- **Breaking:** policy weights are capped at 1,000: rule and band `weight`, and `keywordMatch.requiredWeight`, `preferredWeight`, `responsibilitiesWeight` and `defaultWeight`. Two weights of 1e308 summed to Infinity, so the score read 100 while a category read 0. A larger value, Infinity included, now throws `AtsPolicyError` with the field's path, and `policy.schema.json` carries the cap.
- **Breaking:** a name followed by a job title on the first line is read when one email or one profile link spells every word of it: "Mira Kestrel | Senior Software Engineer", "MIRA KESTREL · Data Analyst" or "Mira Kestrel, Senior Software Engineer" with mira.kestrel@…, mkestrel@… or linkedin.com/in/mira-kestrel. These gave no name. One word is not enough (mira@… alone, or "San Francisco | Software Engineer" with francisco.lopez@…), and a link's address words ("in", "linkedin", "github") never count, so a tagline, a city or an employer is not taken for the name. "Kestrel, Mira" is read as "Mira Kestrel", and "KESTREL, MIRA" as "MIRA KESTREL", under the same condition; otherwise it is no name, so "Java, Spring" or "Munich, Bavaria" names no one, and a state code is never a given name ("Austin, TX"). Either reading gives way to a name on a later line ("Jane Doe" under "Austin, TX" or "Morgan Stanley | Associate" is the name). An employer before the title ("Acme Corp | Senior Engineer") is never the name.
- **Breaking:** an education line split by tab stops or column gaps gives its school: "B.S. Computer Science⇥University of Oregon⇥2018" gives "University of Oregon", not "B.S. Computer Science University of Oregon". A gap after a short joining word, in any case, stays inside the name: "University of", "University Of" or "UNIVERSITY OF", two spaces, then "California, Berkeley" gives "University of California", while "University of Iowa⇥B.S. …" or "BS⇥Stanford University" still splits. In capitals, a short last word before the gap still joins what follows ("UNIVERSITY OF IOWA⇥B.S."), as before. A date range in brackets, spaced or not ("( 2014 - 2018 )"), no longer leaves "( )" or "(" in the school's name.
- **Breaking:** JSON Resume drops empty entries before cutting a list to its 200-entry limit; 200 empty roles in front hid every real one. A file without `basics` (when a role has `position` or a school `institution`), with `basics` that is not an object, or with `work` (or any list) given as one object is read as structure; it was read as plain text, with 0 roles. A `basics` that is only a string is the name when it passes the checks a printed name gets (`"basics": "Richard Hendriks"`, or `"王小明"`); a job title or a heading (`"Senior Software Engineer"`) is not.
- **Breaking:** a job posting is read to its first 20,000 characters on every path. `normalizeJobText`, `jobHtmlToText` and `jobTextFromHtml` cut at 12,000 by default, so a posting from the CLI's `--job` or `--job-url`, the MCP server, or a host using them lost everything past 12,000 while `check` reads 20,000. A 12,681-character posting with its requirements last scored a job match of 36 with no requirements; it now scores 4, with three judged missing. `MAX_JOB_TEXT_CHARS` is 20,000. The helpers cut only when given `maxChars`: pass it if you store or send their output. A longer posting is cut in `check`, and `parsingWarnings` says so.
- A rate limit, server fault, timeout or network error is retried after 1 s, then 2, 4, 8 and 16 s, or after the provider's `retry-after` (decimal seconds or an HTTP date; "0x10", "1e3" or a blank value is ignored, not read as 16 s, 1,000 s or no wait); `retries: 5` made six calls 0 to 1 ms apart. A `retry-after` over a minute is not retried. A call's `signal` ends a wait. `onRetry` receives `delayMs`, and `LlmProviderError` has `retryAfterMs`.
- `timeoutMs` holds when a `fetch` you pass in ignores the abort signal; such a call never returned.
- An error sent with HTTP 200 and no answer (OpenAI-compatible `{"error":…}`, Anthropic `{"type":"error",…}`, Gemini `[{"error":…}]`) is a `provider` error with its message; it read as `invalid_output: Empty response.` and was retried. An answer that comes with an empty `error` (`{}` or `null`), as some gateways add, is still the answer. An error given as a bare string is not retried when it names an invalid key, auth, a permission or something not found ("Invalid API key"), as with an error `type`. Error messages also quote a string `detail` (FastAPI, vLLM), Gemini's error list, and a `message` beside an empty `error.message`, under the same length, line and key-redaction rules.
- On Linux and macOS a path that starts with `//` is read; it was refused as a network share. Windows still refuses shares.
- **Breaking:** hidden text drawn outside its clip is found. A PDF that clips text to an empty or far-away rectangle (`0 0 0 0 re W n`), or places it outside a form's box, had it read as visible: readiness 27 on the audit's sample, now 0. A PDF annotation (a note added with Preview's text tool) is measured as it is drawn: in its own rectangle, with none of the clip, colour or transparency the page set, so a note on a page that clips to its text area is not hidden text. Page text that an annotation's white box covers is. A highlight stays visible: a yellow box multiplied over a line, or one at 40% opacity, is not flagged, nor a highlight pdf.js draws itself, which 0.16.0 flagged. A shape drawn after text in Multiply, Screen, Darken, Lighten, Difference or Exclusion hides it only when, blended, the text is lost against the page: a white Screen box over black text is flagged, a yellow Multiply box is not (0.16.0 flagged a Multiply box drawn after text). A shape in any other blend mode covers as a normal fill does, as in 0.16.0.
- **Breaking:** text whose blend mode leaves the background unchanged is hidden text: black text with Screen or Lighten on the white page, white text with Multiply or Darken on a dark band. Multiply of dark text on a light band, and other honest uses, are unchanged.
- **Breaking:** an image of one colour no longer excuses the text on it. Invisible (`3 Tr`) text over a blank white image is no longer taken for a scan's OCR layer, and white text on a white image (or black on black) is judged against the image's colour. A scanned page with its OCR text over a picture of the words is still not flagged.
- **Breaking:** an image with a soft mask a shade short of opaque (a pixel at 254 of 255) covers the text under it, as an opaque shape does; a mask lets text show only below 90% opacity.
