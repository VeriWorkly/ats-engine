**Breaking:** places named in full and role headers with a department after the title's comma are read right, more look-alike letters are flagged, and the redaction sent with `analyze` catches more phones, so recovered fields and scores change for the same resume.

- **A Greek symbol before a subscript the policy does not list is a look-alike.** "νery" and "ρank" passed the look-alike check as a symbol before a short subscript, as "νmax" does. A lowercase Greek symbol is now excused only before a subscript in the new `text.symbolSubscripts` ("max", "min", "obs", "eff", "cat", "syn" and others); before anything else the word is flagged. Greek words and Greek lines are not, and neither is a word with fewer than three Latin letters, so "Gαs" and "κon" still pass.
- **A phone dialled with an exit prefix is redacted whole.** "011 44 20 7946 0958" (dialled from the US or Canada) was sent to the model as "011 44 [PHONE]". The exit prefixes "011", "0011" (Australia) and "010" (Japan) are now taken with the country code and the number after them, as "00" was; on its own, "011" is left as written.
- **A department after a title's comma stays with the title.** "Director, Product Management | Google" was read as the title "Director" at "Product Management, Google"; it is now "Director, Product Management" at Google. A part made only of the new `resumeParse.departmentWords` ("Product Management", "Research & Development", "Platform") stays with the title before a bar or a dash, whatever the employer is called; before a legal form or an institution's kind it already did. "Software Engineer, Acme | Berlin" is still read as a title, an employer and where. "Engineer, Google, Alphabet Inc." is still read as the title "Engineer, Google": told only by its shape, "Google" there is a team's name ("Engineer, Payments, Acme Corp").
- **A city with its state or country is where a role is worked.** The new `resumeParse.placeNames` names every country, with its common short forms ("USA", "U.S.", "UK", "UAE"), and in full the states and territories of the US, Canada, India and Australia and the UK's nations. "Berlin, Germany", "Austin, Texas, United States", "Bengaluru, Karnataka, India", "Toronto, ON, Canada" and "Remote, India" are now read as places: beside the dates, on a line of their own, or after the employer, so "Software Engineer, Acme Corp, Berlin, Germany" and "Software Engineer | Acme Corp | Bengaluru, Karnataka, India" are at Acme Corp, and "Software Engineer, Acme | Berlin, Germany" is no longer at "Acme, Berlin, Germany". A country after an employer goes ("Infosys | India" is Infosys). On a line of its own, a country or a state names where only when the lines above already name the employer: under a title alone, "Infosys, India" and "Jordan" are still the employer, as is "Infosys, India" over the roles under it. There are no cities: "Acme, Berlin" keeps its city. A first part ending in a legal form is the employer, not a city: "Engineer" over "Acme Corp, TX" had no employer, and is now at Acme Corp. Names that are places' ("Georgia Lane", "Victoria Chen") and employers that hold one ("Georgia Pacific", "Washington Post") read as before. The German pack adds Deutschland, Österreich, Schweiz and the German states, and a pack can add its own `placeNames`.
- **A LinkedIn export's name over a headline with no job title.** In a LinkedIn "Save to PDF" export read sidebar first, a headline with no title word ("Helping fintechs ship faster | Payments | Distributed Systems") left the first top skill ("Machine Learning") as the name, and the name, the headline and the city among the skills. With a LinkedIn profile's address in its contact block, the name is now read as the line over the headline, where the headline is over a place ("Austin, Texas, United States"). A location LinkedIn writes as an area ("Greater Seattle Area") is no place, and leaves the name as before.
