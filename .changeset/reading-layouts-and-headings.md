**Breaking:** more page layouts, headings and role headers are read right, so recovered fields and scores change for the same input.

- **A heading in the left gutter**, printed level with the first line of its section (a moderncv-style page: "EXPERIENCE⇥Senior Software Engineer, Acme Corp⇥Jan 2020 - Present"), opens its section, and the rest of the line is that section's first line. The heading was read as part of the role ("Senior Software Engineer" at "EXPERIENCE, Acme Corp"), the degree beside "EDUCATION" as a job, the skills were lost, and the structure rules found no headings. A heading is read in the gutter only on a page that sets its headings there: two or more, and more than stand on lines of their own. Anywhere else a heading word in a first cell is what it was: a role's title ("Volunteer⇥Red Cross⇥Jan 2016 - Dec 2019", "Internship⇥Acme Labs⇥…"), a line of a role ("Leadership⇥Led 5 engineers") or a row of a skills table ("Training⇥AWS courses"). One beside bare dates ("Volunteer⇥Jan 2016 - Dec 2019") is a title everywhere. Inside Skills, a heading in the gutter other than the work history or education is a category of skills, as one with a colon is.
- **A PDF's running header and footer** are read once, before the text and after it, as a DOCX's are, and its page numbers ("Page 1 of 2") not at all. A line is one when it is among the top or bottom two of a page and printed at the same height on another page, with the same text (a header or footer) or with only its numbers changed (a page number). A later page that opens with the line the first page opens with ("Jordan Ellery - Resume" over the name, on a document whose first page has no header) opens with its header, which is not read either. Left between the pages, they parted a role's title from its dates: "Senior Engineer, Hooli" at the foot of page 1 and its dates at the top of page 2 were read as "Jordan Ellery" at "Resume, jordan.ellery@example.com".
- **A comma inside a job title** is kept: "Director, Product Management | Acme Corp" was read as "Director" at "Product Management, Acme Corp", and "VP, Engineering, Acme Corp" as "VP" at "Engineering, Acme Corp"; each is now the whole title at Acme Corp. The title keeps its comma when a separator stronger than a comma (a bar, a dash, a tab, "at") parts it from a part naming an organisation, or, with commas only, when a later part names one. An organisation is named by a school's word or by the new `resumeParse.organisationWords` (a company's legal form, "Inc", "LLC", "Ltd", "GmbH", "Corp", "Co"; or an institution's kind, "Hospital", "Clinic", "Bank", "Foundation", "Ministry"), which locale packs can extend. Without one, "Software Engineer, Acme | Berlin, Germany" stays a title, an employer and where. The policy schema gains a field, and the policy's fingerprint changes.
- **An employer written before a title** the policy did not know is read the right way round: "Deloitte, Accountant", "Mercy Hospital, Registered Nurse", "Lincoln High School, Teacher", "Acme Corp, CTO" and "Target - Cashier" were read with the employer as the title. The community policy's `titleWords` gain the titles of work beyond the office (nurse, teacher, tutor, instructor, counselor, therapist, pharmacist, physician, accountant, bookkeeper, auditor, attorney, lawyer, paralegal, cashier, teller, clerk, receptionist, secretary, barista, mechanic, electrician, programmer, recruiter, writer, editor, representative, executive, VP, SVP, EVP, CEO, CFO, CTO, COO, CIO), and with no title word on either side, a first part naming an organisation (`organisationWords` or a school's word: "Globex Corporation, Croupier") is the employer. "Account Executive, Head & Shoulders Media", read as "Head & Shoulders Media" at "Account Executive" because only "head" was a title word, is now the account executive at Head & Shoulders Media: the first part with a title word is the title. The policy's fingerprint changes, and these words count wherever title words do: a line holding one is not taken as the name, nor as a heading the policy does not know.
- **Where a role is worked, in brackets after the employer**, is not part of the employer: "Data Scientist, Netflix (Remote)" was read at "Netflix (Remote)", and is now at Netflix, as "Acme Corp (Austin, TX)" is at Acme Corp. A bracket that is not a place stays: "Acme (Europe)".
- **More headings are known**, and one the policy does not know no longer folds what is under it into the skills. The community policy's `resumeParse.sections` read "Selected", "Additional", "Other", "Military" and "Freelance Experience" as the work history; "Technical Proficiencies", "Tech Stack" and "Tools & Technologies" as skills; "Personal", "Academic", "Side", "Selected" and "Key Projects" and "Open Source Projects" or "Contributions" as projects; and "Professional", "Career" and "Executive Summary", "Qualifications", "Summary of Qualifications", "Accomplishments", "Leadership & Activities", "Volunteer Experience" and "Volunteer Work" as sections of their own, which add no months to the work history. A word that is also a skill, a title or a line of a role ("Leadership", "Open Source", "Tools", "Volunteer Firefighter") is not a heading alone. The policy's fingerprint changes.
  - Under a role, a heading not set as the page's headings are (in title case or with a colon on a page of headings in capitals: "Key Projects:", "Accomplishments", "Qualifications", "Tech Stack"), with a dated line after it before the next heading, is a line of the role: the roles after it are not lost.
  - A heading the policy does not know, written like the ones it does, closes Skills as it closes Education, when a dated line comes under it: "Community Involvement" over "Code2040⇥Jan 2018 - Dec 2019" was read as two more skills. A category of skills on a line of its own ("Frameworks" over "React, Vue") still belongs to Skills.
  - Inside the work history or the projects, a skills heading with content after it and a dated line below ("Technologies: React, Node.js", "Tools: Jira, Figma" over the next role) is the role's stack: it opened the Skills section, and every role after it was read as skills.
- **A sidebar taller than the main column**, under a name and headline across the top, is read as a column. Under half of the sidebar's text lay beside the main column, so no gutter was found: `columnRatio` was 0 (the columns rule passed), the lines were read across both columns, a role was read at "jordan.ellery@example.com, Acme Corp", and the skills were lost. The two sides are now beside each other also when the main column lies within the sidebar's height with sidebar text beside it; a centred heading or footer over a short block is still not a column.
- **A scan with a scanner's text stamp** ("Scanned with CamScanner" in a corner) counts as a picture: its 23 characters of text made the page not image-only, so the image-only rule passed and the score was 40 rather than 22. Over an image across most of the page, with no OCR layer (text drawn invisibly), a page with under 200 characters of text is a picture; elsewhere the limit stays 20.
- **A LinkedIn export's name over a long headline** is read: a headline of more than six words ("Senior Software Engineer at Acme Corp | Building payment systems for small businesses") was not taken for one, so a two-word skill in the sidebar ("Machine Learning") was still read as the name. A headline is now judged by the title it opens with. One with no title word ("Helping fintechs ship faster") still is not a headline.
