Mexico, Argentina, Colombia and Chile are read as their own regions, every pack is corrected by a linguist's review, and every miss the bench still listed is read right.

Measured against 0.20.0 on the resumes an independent reviewer wrote for each language (text, DOCX and PDF; detected and with the language given alike): German 99.2% to 100%, Hindi 99.2% to 100%, Spanish 99.3% to 100%, Portuguese 99.3% to 100%, French 100%. No field is read worse. `npm run bench` now also reads every language as a PDF in a Unicode font, and `bench/baseline.json` lists no miss at all: every hand-written resume and every generated one, in English as text, PDF, two-column PDF and DOCX, and in German, French, Hindi, Spanish and Portuguese as text, DOCX and PDF, is read in full. English: on 1,089 runs (every English fixture, 300 generated resumes, the private graded set, review cases, alone and with postings), one resume differs from 0.20.0: its B.Com is read at level 6.

- **Breaking:** new region packs `MX`, `AR`, `CO` and `CL`, all `community`. Each reads its national phone numbers, its state codes after a city ("Monterrey, N.L.", "Santiago, RM", "Bogotá D.C."), its cities, and its schooling: preparatoria (3) and TSU (5) in Mexico; secundario (3) and tecnicatura (5) in Argentina; bachiller (3), técnico profesional and tecnólogo (5) in Colombia; enseñanza media (3) and técnico de nivel superior (5) in Chile. CABA and CDMX are the cities themselves, so "Arcor, CABA" reads employer "Arcor". A date of birth or a photo is not judged there. In 0.20.0 such a resume took Spain's region: an Argentine or Colombian number without its country code went unread.
- **Breaking:** for a resume a language pack reads, the region also comes from its header when the phone has no country code: an email's or site's country domain ("@empresa.com.ar", ".cl"; never ".co" alone, which startups use), else the country's name or one of its cities ("Buenos Aires, Argentina", "Lisboa"). The header ends after the contact line, so a role's city never names it. An English resume is left to its phone number, as before.
- **Breaking:** an honorific before the name is not part of it: "Dr. Miriam Hoffmann", "Prof. Dr. med. Anna Schmidt", "Lic. Ana Ramírez", "Dra. Inês Sousa", "Mme Claire Martin" and "डॉ. अंजलि मिश्रा" read the name alone. A new policy field, `namePrefixes` (English: Dr, Prof, Mr, Mrs, Ms, Mx; each pack adds its own). It is cut only while two name words remain, and undotted capitals are initials ("MS Dhoni" stays).
- **Breaking:** a resume repeats one layout. Once one role reads "Cargo en Empresa S.L." (Spanish "en", Portuguese "na", "no", "em" before a legal form), every role is read that way, the last such word before a comma parting title from employer: "Especialista en Marketing en Cielo Azul Software" reads title "Especialista en Marketing", employer "Cielo Azul Software". A new pack field, `layoutEmployerWords`. Without such a role, "Especialista en Marketing" stays a title.
- **Breaking:** a city beside a role's dates, with the employer and title stacked above it ("Paris 04/2024 à aujourd'hui"), is where the role is worked, not the employer. A city alone with no header above may still be the employer ("Santander").
- **Breaking:** in a PDF, a short run flush with the right margin is a tab stop even without a digit, when at least two in five such lines hold dates: a city on the employer's line over the dates on the title's ("Sahyadri Systems Ltd … पुणे" over "Analyst … 2019 – 2022") is no longer read as a second column, which had cut those roles from their dates.
- **Breaking:** schools and credentials. Fundação Getulio Vargas and Colombia's "Fundación Universitaria" and "Corporación Universitaria" are schools; an institution named by its word alone beside a credential is the school ("Tecnólogo en Logística, SENA, 2013", "INACAP"). B.Com and M.Com are read with no region (Australia, South Africa and Ireland write them too). "BUT Informatique" reads credential "BUT", its speciality the field, as "Diplôme d'ingénieur en Génie logiciel" reads "Diplôme d'ingénieur". "Ausbildung zum Fachinformatiker" keeps its occupation. Portuguese reads "administrador", "gestor", "executivo", "operador", "representante", "secretário", "médico" and "advogado" as titles.
- **Breaking:** a program named by its field alone before a university is the first degree in Spanish and Portuguese, where postgraduate and technologist programs always name themselves: "Administración de Empresas, Universidad de Antioquia" reads level 6. A new pack field, `universityWords` ("universidad", "universidade"); English sets none, so "Computer Science, Stanford University" still reads no level. Spanish "Tecnología en …" and "Técnico Superior/Profesional" read level 5 in any region.
