**Breaking:** requirement and keyword judgements are corrected, so requirement statuses, missing keywords and job match scores change for the same resume and posting.

- **The right to work** is judged on what the resume's statement says, not only on its having one. "Requires H-1B visa sponsorship" met "Must be authorized to work in the US without sponsorship"; it is now missing, with that line as the evidence, wherever the posting rules sponsorship out ("without sponsorship", "not eligible for work sponsorship", "Candidates who need sponsorship will not be considered"), and is never met elsewhere. So is a line that needs it later ("Not currently requiring sponsorship, but will require sponsorship in the future"). "Authorized to work in the US; no sponsorship required" and "U.S. citizen, does not require visa sponsorship" were unverifiable because of their "no" and "not"; they now meet it, while "Cannot work in the US without visa sponsorship" still does not. An ask for U.S. citizenship alone ("Must be a U.S. citizen") is met only by citizenship held: "Green card holder" or "Permanent resident" is now missing, "U.S. citizenship pending" and "Former U.S. citizen" are no evidence, and "U.S. citizen or green card holder" accepts either. A resume that says nothing about it is still unverifiable.
- **A clearance** is compared by level. "Active Secret clearance" met "Active Top Secret clearance required"; it is now partial, with a detail ("Secret read, Top Secret asked"). A higher level meets a lower ask, the lowest level an ask names is its bar ("Secret or Top Secret"), and a clearance whose level the resume does not state is partial against an ask that names one ("No level read, Top Secret asked"). "Confidential clearance", "TS clearance", "TS-SCI" and "Top Secret/SCI" are now read as clearances. A clearance the resume is "eligible for", has "pending" or is "able to obtain", or held "formerly", is no evidence, as an "inactive" or "expired" one was.
- **"Minimum"** and "min" are stopwords. "Minimum of 5 years of software engineering experience" was partial with "0 years in roles naming minimum, software, engineering", since no role names "minimum"; it now counts the roles naming software engineering, and "minimum" leaves the missing keywords.
- **Software development**: "development", "developer" and "developed" fold to "develop", and a software engineer's role is read as software development (`implies: { "software engineer": ["software", "engineer", "development"] }`, with "software engineer" and "business development" added to `phrases`). "5+ years of hands-on software development experience" and "5+ years of experience as a software developer" were partial with "0 years in roles naming software, development" for a resume of Software Engineer roles; they are now met. Another kind of engineer is not a developer: a Sales Engineer's years do not meet "software development", nor any engineer's "business development".
- **A silent "e"** folds with the "-ing", "-ed" and "-es" forms of its word. "nursing" and "nurse", "pricing" and "price", "scheduling" and "schedule", "trading" and "trade", "closing" and "close", "managing" and "manage", "housing" and "house" did not meet; a nurse's roles counted 0 years toward "3+ years of nursing experience" and now count 8. "Focused" now meets "focus". "Accounting" no longer meets "account" nor "marketing" "market": an Account Executive's years were counted as accounting experience. "Experienced", "requires", "includes", "relates" and "demonstrates" now fold onto stopwords and leave the keyword lists, as "experience", "required", "including" and "related" did. A few unrelated words now fold together too: "state" and "stat", "locale" and "local", "plane" and "plan", "Stripe" and "strip", "grade" and "grad", "paste" and "past". The rules are the policy's `stemming` defaults; a rule that gives a word back unchanged ("accounting") keeps the rules after it off that word.
- **A slash** between skills names each of them. "HTML/CSS", "C#/.NET" and "Python/Django" were one keyword each, so a resume listing "HTML, CSS" missed "HTML/CSS" and the other way round. Each part is now its own keyword and its own term of a requirement, all of them asked: "HTML/CSS" is met by HTML and CSS and partly met by HTML alone, as AWS alone partly meets "AWS/GCP/Azure"; beside an "or" each part is one of the choices, so React alone meets "React/Redux or Vue". A term the policy writes with its slash stays whole: "CI/CD" as before, and "TCP/IP", "PL/SQL", "A/B", "I/O" and "TS/SCI", now in `phrases`. So do "N/A" (now a stopword, as is "off") and a compound with a part that is no word ("OS/2", "24/7"). "and/or" and "his/her" split into stopwords ("his", "her", "him" and "she" are new ones) and leave the keyword lists. A link ("https://careers.example.com/jobs/123") is no longer read for keywords.
- Policy schema: `keywordMatch.requirements` gains `needsSponsorship`, `noSponsorship`, `negation`, `notHeld`, `citizenship` and `residence` (word lists) and `clearanceLevels` (named pattern lists, lowest first: Confidential, Secret, Top Secret, TS/SCI), all defaulted. The negation words were fixed in the engine before; they are now the `negation` list, with "cannot", "unable" and contractions ("can't") added.
